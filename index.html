Axel Ridberg is a sound designer based in Helsinki, Finland. Working primarily within performing arts. Also short films, commercial work, installations, live mixing, creative coding, and other sound-related projects.

He has a master’s and bachelor’s degree in sound design from the Theatre Academy at the University of the Arts Helsinki, a bachelor’s degree in social sciences from Åbo Akademi University, and has also studied film and television sound at Arcada University of Applied Sciences. His work has been presented at e.g. Helsinki City Theatre, Teater Viirus, Q-teatteri, Reality Research Center, and at the Fast Forward Festival.

Steal This Performance

Method-based performance created by plagiarising other performances.

Steal This Performance explores questions of originality and identity. Every element on stage—including the sound design—is constructed entirely from stolen, plagiarised, or otherwise borrowed material, most of it from other performances and theatre shows. While the work takes the form of a monologue, it relies heavily on audiovisual and scenographic means to articulate its core idea.

The production premiered at the Theatre Academy in Helsinki in 2024. It was later selected for the Fast Forward Festival 2025 in Dresden, Germany, where it received the Jury Award.

Credits: Directing & Dramaturgy Pauli Patinen. On stage: Juho Keränen. Scenography Anni Hernetkoski. Lighting & Video Design Ville Tolvanen. Sound Design Axel Ridberg. Costume Design Ellen Järnefelt. Stagehands Anni Hernetkoski, Mikael Karkkonen.

Kärnminne

Installation in a 700 m2 abandonded bowling hall for ParKino Festival.

Kärnminne (Core Memory) refers to formative experiences and memories. The installation tries to capture the fluid nature of these moments and the way they are stored in our heads. It consisted of hundreds of A4 papers, a video installation, multi-channel audio, and lighting that interacted with the papers to create shifting shadow formations. After opening hours, the installation could be viewed through the windows in the space.

Credits: Concept & execution Axel Ridberg. Exhibition help Ida Ridberg. Production Ida Ridberg, Liisa Taulio, Sebastian Allén. Special thanks Tony Nurmi Samu Kotilainen Pauli Kotilainen

Diptyk

Theatre show about social choreographies around death in western societies.

A diptych is a work of art consisting of two separate parts that, when combined, form a unified work of art. These parts can be attached or displayed side by side.

The first part of the performance explores the social and spatial choreographies of a good death in Western society. The second part asks the question of what we can do in this moment, while we’re alive?

Diptyk places itself in a continuum of artworks about death, raising questions about the medicalisation before death, care, and our ability to deal with death and community.
